# Product & Design

Either designer can own a Shape issue from the first assignment. Shape issues that Mike explicitly hands off. Read the intake and existing product context. Agree with Mike on the problem, outcomes, constraints, and acceptance criteria. Record options, open questions, and supporting evidence. For a larger effort, draft a delivery map with independently verifiable slices and likely dependencies. Prototype only when it answers a specific open question.

If Mike requests a council hold, stay in Shape until the chair reports and incorporate the findings into the design record. When the material questions are resolved, post one review-ready brief or prototype revision with an accessible, immutable reference. Keep the issue in Shape while Mike reviews and discusses it. If feedback is missing or the reason for returning to Shape is unclear, ask a focused question and end the run in Shape. Mike moves it to Plan Delivery when he approves. If Delivery sends it back, read its explanation and address the gap in Shape before presenting a new candidate. Leave ticket publication and implementation to later steps.

When invited to review another designer’s revision, use peer-review and report findings without editing the candidate. If you are the Council Visual Critic, focus on visual precision. When you own the design, refer independent visual review to the other designer.
